    Prologue

    Christopher Nolan makes films about time. He tells them backwards, folds them inside dreams, bends them around a black hole and runs them in reverse. This page is built the same way. Your scroll is the projector, and the timecode in the corner keeps count.

    Life

    1970

    Born on 30 July in Westminster, London. His father, Brendan, is a British advertising executive; his mother, Christina Jensen, an American flight attendant who later teaches English. He grows up between London and Chicago and holds both citizenships.

    1977

    Aged seven, he borrows his father's Super 8 camera and makes stop-motion films with his toy action figures. Around the same age he sees 2001: A Space Odyssey on a big screen in London, and never gets over it.

    1989

    Tarantella, a surreal short made with his friend Roko Belic, is shown on Image Union, a public-television showcase for independent film in Chicago.

    1993

    Graduates in English literature from University College London. With Emma Thomas he runs the college film society, screening 35mm prints during term and spending the takings on 16mm shorts in the summer.

    1997

    Marries Emma Thomas, who will produce his films. Makes Doodlebug, a three-minute short about a man in a flat chasing a tiny version of himself with a shoe.

    1998

    Following, his first feature, shot on 16mm black-and-white film over about a year of Saturdays for roughly $6,000.

    2001

    Memento becomes a sleeper hit in America. He and Emma Thomas found the production company Syncopy.

    2008

    The Dark Knight shoots six sequences, 28 minutes, with IMAX cameras, the first Hollywood feature to do so, and passes $1 billion worldwide.

    2010

    Inception, an original story he had developed for years, earns eight Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture.

    2017

    Dunkirk, about three quarters of it shot with IMAX cameras, brings his first nomination for Best Director.

    2018

    At Cannes he presents an unrestored 70mm print of 2001: A Space Odyssey for its 50th anniversary, struck from the original camera negative. The film he saw at seven.

    2019

    Appointed CBE in the Birthday Honours, for services to film.

    2023

    Oppenheimer is shot partly on black-and-white IMAX film, a stock Kodak made for the first time for this film.

    2024

    Wins the Academy Award for Best Director and, with Emma Thomas and Charles Roven, Best Picture. Knighted in the New Year Honours; Emma Thomas is made a Dame.

    2025

    Elected the 29th president of the Directors Guild of America on 20 September, unopposed. He has been a member since 2001.

    2026

    The Odyssey, the first feature shot entirely with IMAX film cameras, opens on 17 July and passes $1 billion worldwide within three weeks.

    House rules

    On a Nolan set

    1. No chairs.Actors and crew have described sets with nowhere to sit, to keep the energy up.
    2. No phones.Phones stay off the set. He has said he does not carry a smartphone or use email.
    3. Earl Grey.A thermos of tea, all day, in place of coffee.
    4. Film, not digital.He shoots on photochemical film, large format where he can, and fights to keep film prints in cinemas.
    5. Real, where possible.A truck flipped on a Chicago street, a 747 crashed into a building, a nuclear test rebuilt without computer graphics.

    01199869 min16mm, black and white

    Following

    A writer follows strangers for material. One of them turns around.

    A struggling young writer follows strangers through London to find characters, until a burglar named Cobb notices him and takes him along on his break-ins.

    Nolan shot Following on weekends over about a year, with friends who had day jobs, on 16mm black-and-white stock. Every scene was rehearsed until it could be filmed in one or two takes, because the film stock was the budget.

    The story is cut from three timelines, and the young man's look tells you which one you are in: long hair, then a suit and a short cut, then a bruised face. The burglar is called Cobb; twelve years later Nolan gave the name to Leonardo DiCaprio's thief in Inception. A Batman logo is stuck to the young man's door.

    022000113 minColour and black and white

    Memento

    The ending comes first. This title is running backwards.

    Leonard Shelby cannot make new memories. He hunts his wife's killer with Polaroids, handwritten notes and tattoos, and trusts none of the people who help him.

    The film runs in two directions at once. The colour scenes play in reverse order, each one ending where the previous one began, so you know as little as Leonard does. The black-and-white scenes run forwards. At the end they meet: a Polaroid develops, and the black and white turns to colour.

    Based on Memento Mori, a short story by his brother Jonathan Nolan. Shot in 25 days for $9 million, it was nominated for Academy Awards for its screenplay and its editing, and in 2017 the Library of Congress added it to the National Film Registry.

    032002118 minNightmute, Alaska

    Insomnia

    In an Alaskan summer, the sun never sets.

    Two Los Angeles detectives fly to a small Alaskan town to investigate a murder. Under a sun that never goes down, one of them stops sleeping.

    A remake of Erik Skjoldbjærg's 1997 Norwegian thriller. Al Pacino plays Will Dormer, who shoots his own partner in the fog; Robin Williams plays the crime writer who saw it happen; Hilary Swank plays the local detective who idolises Dormer.

    It is the only Nolan feature he did not write: the screenplay is by Hillary Seitz. His first studio film, it was made with the team from Memento: cinematographer Wally Pfister, editor Dody Dorn, composer David Julyan.

    042005140 minGotham, built in a hangar

    Batman Begins

    To conquer fear, you must become fear.

    After years abroad learning to master his fear, Bruce Wayne returns to a city run by criminals and turns that fear against them.

    Nolan rebuilt the character from the ground up, asking how a real man could do it. The Tumbler was a working vehicle built from scratch. Gotham's streets were built inside a former airship hangar at Cardington, in Bedfordshire, and the ice training was shot on the Vatnajökull glacier in Iceland.

    It began his partnership with Hans Zimmer, who scored it with James Newton Howard, and with editor Lee Smith. Michael Caine played Alfred, the first of his eight Nolan films.

    052006130 minLondon, Colorado Springs

    The Prestige

    Are you watching closely?

    Two magicians in Victorian London, once partners, destroy their careers, their marriages and each other to perform the better version of one trick.

    Christopher and Jonathan Nolan adapted Christopher Priest's 1995 novel and built the film like a magic trick in three acts: the pledge, the turn and the prestige. It hides its secret in plain sight from the first scene.

    David Bowie plays Nikola Tesla, whose machine in Colorado Springs leaves behind a field of identical top hats. The film was nominated for Academy Awards for its art direction and its photography.

    The Dark Knight

    Why so serious?

    35mm anamorphic · 2.39 : 1

    IMAX 15/70 · 1.43 : 1

    The frame opens for the bank heist. 28 minutes of the film were shot with IMAX cameras, a first for a Hollywood feature.

    Batman, Lieutenant Gordon and the new district attorney Harvey Dent set out to dismantle Gotham's mob, until the Joker arrives with no plan at all.

    Nolan shot six sequences on IMAX film, starting with the opening bank heist. The truck flip on Chicago's LaSalle Street was done for real. Heath Ledger's Joker won a posthumous Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor; the film also won for sound editing, from eight nominations.

    It became Nolan's first film to pass $1 billion worldwide. When it missed a Best Picture nomination, the Academy expanded the category from five films to ten the following year.

    072010148 minFour levels deep

    Inception

    An idea is the most resilient parasite.

    A thief who steals secrets from people's dreams is offered his way home in exchange for the opposite job: planting an idea.

    Each dream level runs about twenty times slower than the one above it. Ten hours on a flight from Sydney to Los Angeles become a week, then six months, then ten years. The rotating hallway fight was shot for real, in a corridor built to turn like a wheel.

    Hans Zimmer built the score's brass from a slowed-down Édith Piaf song, Non, je ne regrette rien, the same song the team plays to signal the kick. The film won four Academy Awards: cinematography, visual effects, sound mixing and sound editing.

    082012164 minMore than an hour in IMAX

    The Dark Knight Rises

    The legend ends.

    Eight years after the Joker, a broken Bruce Wayne comes back to face Bane, who cuts Gotham off from the world and holds it hostage.

    Nolan edited more than an hour of IMAX footage into the film. The opening hijacking of a plane in mid-air was shot with real aircraft over Scotland, and the stadium sequence was filmed at Pittsburgh's Heinz Field with thousands of extras.

    Thrown into a prison at the bottom of a well, Bruce climbs out the only way anyone has: without the rope, with the other prisoners chanting. It closed the trilogy at $1.08 billion worldwide.

    092014169 minGravity bends time

    Interstellar

    Mankind was born on Earth. It was never meant to die here.

    As blight kills Earth's crops, a former NASA pilot leaves his children to search for a new home for humanity, through a wormhole near Saturn.

    Physicist Kip Thorne, who later shared the Nobel Prize, was scientific adviser and executive producer. The visual effects company Double Negative rendered the black hole Gargantua from his equations, and the work produced peer-reviewed papers on gravitational lensing. For clarity, the film's version leaves out the Doppler shift that would make one side of the disc far brighter.

    Nolan grew 500 acres of corn for the farm scenes. Hans Zimmer recorded the score on the organ of the Temple Church in London. The film won the Academy Award for visual effects.

    102017106 minLand, sea, air

    Dunkirk Dunkirk Dunkirk Air · 1 hourSea · 1 dayMole · 1 week

    When 400,000 men couldn't get home, home came for them.

    May 1940. Allied soldiers are trapped on the beach at Dunkirk with the enemy closing in. The story is told from the land, the sea and the air.

    Three timelines of three different lengths are cut together so that they converge at the end: The Mole, one week; The Sea, one day; The Air, one hour. Shot on the real beach at Dunkirk with real ships and Spitfires, about three quarters of it on IMAX cameras. At 106 minutes it was his shortest film since Following.

    Hans Zimmer built the score on a Shepard tone, the illusion of a pitch that rises forever, and on the ticking of Nolan's own pocket watch. Eight Academy Award nominations, his first for Best Director; three wins, for editing, sound editing and sound mixing.

    112020150 minTimecode now runs backwards

    TENET

    Don't try to understand it. Feel it.

    A secret agent with one word, Tenet, is drawn into a war with the future, fought with objects and people that move backwards through time.

    Inverted bullets fly back into guns, inverted cars drive in reverse, and some fights run forwards and backwards in the same shot. To film a plane crashing into a building, Nolan bought a real Boeing 747, which he said was cheaper than miniatures and digital effects.

    It opened in August 2020, the first Hollywood blockbuster released into cinemas during the COVID-19 pandemic. It was his first film with composer Ludwig Göransson and editor Jennifer Lame, and it won the Academy Award for visual effects.

    122023180 minFission · Fusion

    Oppenheimer

    The world forever changes.

    The physicist J. Robert Oppenheimer leads the Manhattan Project to build the atomic bomb, then watches the country he served turn on him.

    Adapted from American Prometheus, the Pulitzer Prize-winning biography by Kai Bird and Martin J. Sherwin. Nolan wrote Oppenheimer's scenes in the first person. They play in colour, titled Fission; the black-and-white scenes, titled Fusion, follow Lewis Strauss from the outside. Kodak made black-and-white IMAX film stock for the first time for it.

    The Trinity test was recreated without computer graphics. Released on 21 July 2023, the same day as Barbie, it took $975 million worldwide and won seven Academy Awards from thirteen nominations: Picture, Director, Actor for Cillian Murphy, Supporting Actor for Robert Downey Jr., cinematography, editing and score.

    The Odyssey

    Ten years of war. Ten years home.

    After the fall of Troy, Odysseus spends ten years trying to sail home to Ithaca, to his wife Penelope and the son he has never known.

    Nolan's adaptation of Homer is the first feature shot entirely with IMAX film cameras. Ludwig Göransson's score leaves out the orchestra, which did not exist in Homer's world, in favour of ancient instruments such as the aulos and the lyre.

    Released by Universal on 17 July 2026, it opened to $264 million worldwide, the biggest opening of Nolan's career, and passed $1 billion within three weeks. Bill Irwin, the voice of TARS in Interstellar, is in the cast.
